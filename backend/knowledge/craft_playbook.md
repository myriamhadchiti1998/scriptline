# CRAFT PLAYBOOK — client-agnostic direct-response video knowledge

This file is shared by every client. It holds *how to write*, never *facts about
a client*. Client facts live only in the client's Reference Pack and proof bank.

## 1. Short-form video ad anatomy (A-roll)

An A-roll script is what the on-camera talent says. Runtime target 60–180 seconds.
At a calm delivery (~150 words per minute) that is roughly 150–450 spoken words,
and the sweet spot for most cold ads is 75–150 seconds (≈190–380 words).

```
HOOK (0–3s)        → stop the scroll; one of three interchangeable openers
PAIN (≈15–25%)     → name the problem in the viewer's own words; make them feel seen
PROMISE (≈15–20%)  → the reframe + unique mechanism: why THIS is different, structurally
PROOF (≈25–35%)    → credibility the viewer could check; process proof > outcome claims
CTA (≈10–15%)      → one clear next step matched to funnel stage; low friction
```

Pain → Promise → Proof → CTA is a strict order. Each section has one job:
- **Pain** ends when the viewer has nodded at least once ("that's me").
- **Promise** must name the *mechanism*, not just the outcome. "Here's why it's
  built differently" beats "here's what you'll get".
- **Proof** stacks several checkable elements (credentials, scale, structure,
  named member stories) — never invented ones.
- **CTA** says exactly what to do, what it costs, and what happens next.

## 2. Hooks that land in the first 3 seconds

The first 6–10 spoken words decide the scroll. A pattern interrupt is anything
the viewer did not expect from an ad in this category:

| Pattern interrupt | Shape |
|---|---|
| Contrarian admission | Say the opposite of what every competitor says. |
| Callout of the private thought | Voice the exact sentence the viewer thinks but never says out loud. |
| Unexpected specific | A concrete, odd, true detail (a place, an object, a number from the proof bank). |
| Direct challenge | "Don't trust me. Check this instead." |
| Mid-story open | Start inside a moment, not before it. |
| Question that implicates | A question the viewer can't answer "no" to without lying. |

Rules: the first sentence is short (ideally under 12 words, never over 20); no
throat-clearing ("Hey guys", "In this video"); the three hooks for one script
must open three *different* doors into the same body (different interrupt type
or different emotional entry), not three rewordings.

## 3. Open loops (watch-time)

Plant a question early and close it late. Examples of shape (not copy):
"There's one question nobody in this space wants you to ask. I'll get to it."
"…which brings me back to the question I said I'd answer." Every script needs
at least one deliberate open loop, and it must be closed before or in the CTA.

## 4. Creative types

- **talking-head** — founder/spokesperson to camera, single location.
- **founder-confessional** — talking-head in first person, story-led ("I spent…").
- **UGC** — a member-style voice. Only usable with real, named proof-bank
  testimonials; never invent a member.
- **listicle** — "3 questions everyone asks…" numbered structure.
- **story** — narrative arc with a turning point.
- **demo** — show the mechanism on screen (sheet, listing, template).
- **objection-handler** — opens on the objection, dismantles it with structure.

## 5. Lead types

- **problem-led** — opens on the viewer's pain or private thought.
- **proof-led** — opens on a checkable credential or structural fact.
- **curiosity-led** — opens on a gap the viewer needs closed.
- **offer-led** — opens on the low-friction offer (best for retargeting).

## 6. ICE scoring rubric (1–10 each)

**Impact** — if this concept wins, how much does it move the client's goal?
- 9–10: hits the #1 burning problem AND the dominant limiting belief for a high-value avatar; would plausibly scale as a control.
- 6–8: strong emotional entry for a valid avatar; clear mechanism tie-in.
- 3–5: on-brief but generic; the angle exists in many competitor ads.
- 1–2: weak or off-avatar.

**Confidence** — how sure are we it will work, given the evidence we hold?
- 9–10: built on a hook/angle the Reference Pack identifies as working, with strong proof available, zero compliance risk.
- 6–8: sound reasoning and good proof, but partially untested angle or moderate compliance sensitivity.
- 3–5: speculative; leans on weaker proof; or near a compliance line.
- 1–2: contradicts the pack's guidance (e.g. procedural hook for cold traffic).

**Ease** — how cheap and fast is it to produce well?
- 9–10: single talking-head take, no props, no new assets.
- 6–8: needs simple B-roll or screen capture (sheet, template, garage bins).
- 3–5: needs a member on camera, multiple locations, or heavy edit.
- 1–2: needs assets that don't exist yet.

ICE total = mean of the three. Reasoning must name the specific evidence behind
each number, so a human approver can disagree with it in one read.
