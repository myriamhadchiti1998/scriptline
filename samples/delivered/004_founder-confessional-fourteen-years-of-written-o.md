## Hooks

**Hook 1 — pattern interrupt · unexpected specific**

I spent fourteen years in a depot watching perfectly good returned stock get written off. Nobody told the people at home who wanted something of their own.

**Hook 2 — alternate angle · private thought callout**

You used to be good at something. Somewhere along the way you started wondering if a real thing of your own is for other people.

**Hook 3 — third option · contrarian admission**

I'm not going to tell you a number. Anyone who does is either lying or about to be sued.

## Main body

**PAIN**

Let me tell you where I'm coming from. Maybe you've been burned. You saw the red flags and wanted it to work anyway, and now you don't trust your own judgment. You used to be good at something. You want a thing that's yours again, something you could be proud of, and you're quietly wondering if that's for people like you. So you ask the obvious question: what's the catch this time? Every one of these has one. I'll tell you mine before we're done.

**PROMISE**

Here's what I built, and it's boring on purpose. Regional liquidators always have overstock and returned inventory they need to clear. They pay me a placement fee when a member's store actually moves it. Members don't pay me up front for results. My income depends on your store working. Then there's the match sheet, refreshed weekly: lots the partners most need to clear, matched to store types. So the demand exists before your store does. The work is a few evenings a week, with a cataloguing system and templates for listing, pricing, and returns. I won't insult you by calling it passive.

**PROOF**

Now check it, because you should. I managed a regional wholesale logistics depot for 14 years. I have 11 vetted regional liquidator partners under signed placement agreements. The program's been running five years, and the weekly match sheets have run that whole time. There are 9,400+ enrolled members across the US, Canada, the UK, and Australia. I won't give you an income number, because anyone who does shouldn't be trusted. Marisol V., a parent in Arizona, says: "I've got a little corner of the garage with labeled bins and a store with my name on it." Ray C. in Canada says: "The catch is that you have to actually do the weekly work." That's my catch. The weekly work is yours.

**CTA**

If that's the kind of real, boring thing you want, watch the First Pallet workshop for $1. It's two hours, live, and it walks the whole mechanism end to end. See exactly how it works, then decide.
