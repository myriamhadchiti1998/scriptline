## Hooks

**Hook 1 — pattern interrupt · direct challenge**

Don't take my word for it. Go read how liquidator placement works first. Then come back and check who writes my check.

**Hook 2 — alternate angle · private thought callout**

You're not watching this to be convinced. You're watching to find the hole in it. Good. Keep looking.

**Hook 3 — third option · unexpected specific**

Eleven signed agreements. That's what sits behind this. Not a promise, a count you can hold me to.

## Main body

**PAIN**

You've been burned before, so you read the fine print now. You want the incentives to line up. You want the economics walked through, not promised. There is a catch. I'll tell you what it is before I ask you for anything.

**PROMISE**

Don't ask whether this works. Ask who pays whom. Regional liquidators have overstock and returned inventory they need to clear. They pay me a placement fee, but only when a member's store moves it. Members don't pay me up front for results. If your store doesn't move product, I don't get paid. Every week, members get the Liquidator Match sheet: overstock and returned lots the partners most need to clear, matched to store types. So the demand exists before your store does. The weekly work is cataloguing, listing, pricing and returns, with a done-with-you system and templates.

**PROOF**

Now the part you can hold me to. I managed a regional wholesale logistics depot for 14 years. I have 11 vetted regional liquidator partners under signed placement agreements. I've run this program for 5 years, and the weekly match sheets have run continuously that whole time. 9,400+ members across the US, Canada, the UK, and Australia. And here's the catch I promised. Ray C., an analyst in Canada, put it better than I could: "I went in looking for the catch. The catch is that you have to actually do the weekly work. The economics hold up, which is more than I can say for the last three." That's the catch. The work is yours, a few evenings a week, and I won't call it passive. I won't give you a number either. Anyone who does shouldn't be trusted.

**CTA**

The First Pallet workshop is two hours, live, and it walks the full mechanism end to end, so you can check every step of what I just said. You also get a sample Liquidator Match sheet and the Boring Economics workbook, the unit math of a single moved lot, no projections. Watch the First Pallet workshop for $1. See exactly how it works, then decide.
