02 — CLIENT IDENTITY

Who Marcus Is
Marcus Vane spent 14 years managing a regional wholesale logistics depot before building a reselling education business. He is positioned as the un-guru: no sports cars, no rented mansions, no urgency theatre. The brand personality is the operator who got tired of watching people get sold a fantasy and decided to teach the unglamorous version that actually holds up. He leans into the audience's skepticism, explains the economics openly, and lets the mechanism carry the sale.

Origin Story
The online-business education space is full of programs selling "passive" stores stuffed with random imported products nobody asked for. Marcus spent over a decade on the warehouse side of retail and knew the unsexy truth: there is a constant, predictable river of overstock and customer-returned inventory that regional liquidators need to clear, and they will pay placement fees to move it through reliable small storefronts. So he flipped the usual model. Instead of charging students thousands to guess at "winning products," he built relationships with vetted liquidators who pay him when student storefronts actually move their inventory. The incentives are aligned by structure: if students' stores do not move product, Marcus does not get paid.

3. Cost-of-entry fear. They cannot stomach losing another few thousand dollars on inventory that does not move. The placement-fee model and the $1 trial cap the downside.

09 — LOSING SCRIPTS (NEGATIVE EXAMPLES)

These are deliberately off-brand and non-compliant. They are included so the system can be taught what NOT to produce. A correct pipeline should never generate anything resembling these, and the quality review should reject them on the cited grounds.

Losing Script 1 — The Income-Claim Disaster.
"What if I told you that you could make $5,000 a month flipping pallets from your couch? Dana did it in her FIRST WEEK with zero experience! Marcus's students are pulling in $300/day on autopilot. This is the easiest passive income on the planet. Spots are almost gone, click now before the timer hits zero!!!"

Why it fails: Invents earnings claims ($5,000/month, $300/day) — banned outright. Fabricates a result for Dana not in the proof bank. Uses "passive," "autopilot," "easiest." Fake urgency timer. All-caps hype. Auto-reject.

Losing Script 2 — The Fabricated-Proof Script.
"Marcus was a Wall Street trader who left to teach reselling. He's got 40,000 students and partnerships with Walmart and Amazon directly. As seen on Forbes and Shark Tank. One student, Greg, turned $100 into a six-figure empire in 90 days."
Why it fails: Fabricates the origin (he was a logistics manager, not a trader), inflates scale (9,400, not 40,000), invents partners (never named in ads, and never Walmart/Amazon), invents media placements and a student "Greg" with an income result. Zero-tolerance failure.

Losing Script 3 — The Off-Voice Hype Script.
"Tired of being broke? It's time to FIRE YOUR BOSS and live the laptop lifestyle! Marcus is handing you the keys to a money-printing machine. Imagine the Lamborghini, the beach, the freedom. Stop being a wage slave. The only thing standing between you and millions is this button."
Why it fails: Pure guru energy, lambo/lifestyle imagery, "fire your boss," "millions" — the opposite of the calm, anti-hype, small-dream voice. Implies an income claim. Auto-reject.

10 — COPY GAPS & COMPLIANCE FLAGS

NHB Gaps That Need Work
• Hopeless under-addressed. Needs more "I was burned AND this was different" proof, not just the mechanism explanation.
• Skepticism-to-trust arc not mapped in testimonials. Need "I was sure it was a scam, then I watched the workshop" process stories.
• Age-diverse proof gap. No member testimonials explicitly 60+, the Hopeless-breaker for Lorraine.
• Low-effort honesty. "A few evenings a week" should be a recurring refrain so no one feels misled later.
• Under-addressed objections: "What makes yours different from the 20 other reseller ads?", "What if the matched lot doesn't sell?", "Am I on my own after I get the match sheet?"

Compliance Hard Rules
• No income or earnings claims, ever. No dollar figures tied to what a member could make. No earnings-based testimonials.
• Never name a specific liquidator partner in ads; refer to "vetted regional partners."
• Do not imply Marcus earns from the member directly; the model is liquidators pay him on movement.
• No hype, guru energy, get-rich-quick language, lambo/mansion imagery.

• Platform policy compliance (Facebook, YouTube, TikTok) at all times.
• Members are called "members," never students or customers.
• Never promise "passive." Always frame as "a few evenings a week."

Competitor Positioning Quick Reference
• Dropshipping gurus: "They make money when you pay. He makes money when the liquidators pay him, only if your store moves product."
• Amazon FBA: "FBA wants capital and months before you know if it works. This caps your downside at a dollar to look."
• Free flip-it content: "Free advice is great if you have 200 hours and no reliable supply. Or you watch one workshop and start with inventory that already has demand."
